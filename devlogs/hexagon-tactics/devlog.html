A note on the downloadable builds of Hexagon Tactics: real-time generation no longer works. The game is still fully playable, but it now uses the pre-generated fire-witch world instead of creating your own. Here's what changed and why.

What's changed

The Windows and Mac downloads originally let you describe your own world and hero, then generated everything live while you played: your backstory, your portrait, every card's name and picture, and the enemies who live in your world. That relied on several online services we ran for the jam:

  • Falcon-7B-Instruct on a Hugging Face inference endpoint for names, backstories and descriptions, with GPT-3.5 as a fallback.
  • Stable Diffusion 1.5 on a rented GPU server for every character and card image.
  • Background removal, and text-to-speech for voices.

Those services are no longer running, so character creation and live generation in the downloadable builds don't work any more. If you try to create a new world, the generation calls won't come back.

What still works

Near the jam deadline we shipped a complete pre-generated set with the game, so it would keep working when the servers were off. That set is what you'll play now: the fire-witch hero, her full deck of 24 cards and all eight enemy types, with their art and descriptions. The tactics are unchanged: the 5-Power turns, the expanding hex arena, the card picks between waves and the climb to the 500 HP boss.

The pre-generated deck you'll play with now.

Why not keep it running?

Hosting a language model and a GPU for image generation costs money every hour they're on, and the 2023 models and endpoints we built on have moved on. Keeping them alive for a jam game wasn't sustainable. One player put it best after we turned the servers off: "Oh... I so wish I could generate my characters and such, what a shame." I agree, and it's the clearest lesson I took from the project: every generated game needs an offline mode. The caching and the shipped default set are the only reason this one is still playable.

Could it come back?

Possibly. Today's image models generate in about a second and structured output would fix a lot of the rough edges (like the "fast moving fighter" that became an F-16). If I rebuild it, I'll write about it here. In the meantime, the full story of how the pipeline worked, what went wrong and how I'd do it now is on my portfolio: Hexagon Tactics: The Expanding Arena.
