01

The path

Six chapters, from social games to VR to AI.

2012–2017

Zynga and the first startups

  • I started in games at Zynga in 2012 and worked on the Ville games, FrontierVille and FarmVille 3 on mobile.
  • A group of us left Zynga and started our own companies.
  • I made a game at my own company that was funded on Kickstarter.
  • One of the others was doing well, so we all joined it, and it was sold for a little over $100M.
  • That was Rocket Games. I was a System Designer there, but really I was the person who took on the hard, unknown challenges.

2017–2018

VR, and my first game with AI

  • After the sale we split up again, and I got into VR development.
  • In 2018 I made my first game that contained AI.
  • I led a team to make Flicksync, a concept from Ready Player One.
  • We won two of five categories at the Creating Reality hackathon, including best use of AI and best entertainment app.

2018–2020

Coinflip Studios and Ninja Legends

  • I bet the studio on the Oculus Quest before it was out, and being early paid off.
  • Several groups from Rocket Games formed new companies. Mine became Coinflip Studios, and we rapidly iterated on mobile titles and Facebook Messenger games.
  • Then I went to Oculus Connect, saw the Oculus Quest, and convinced the team to switch to it as a blue-ocean opportunity.
  • I forged connections at Oculus and got us Quest dev kits and a spot on the early roadmap. Ninja Legends launched in the first set of post-launch games, and did well. (Quest store · Oculus blog interview)

2020–2025

Oculus and Meta Reality Labs

  • The team took a mobile game with Crunchyroll. I wanted to stay in VR, so I joined Oculus as a Senior Product Design Prototyper.
  • On the Guardian team, which used a lot of interpretive AI: hand tracking, detecting instructions in the playspace, and detecting objects like tables. (Room setup case study)
  • Moved to the Orion team, started working with generative AI, and moved to London with them. (Orion messaging · Object locking AI)
  • Became known as the prototyper who worked the most with AI, and presented on AI to the group at the 2023 and 2024 prototyping conferences.
  • Built tools so every other prototyper could easily use AI integrations.
  • Rated Exceeds Expectations in every performance review, and Greatly Exceeds Expectations in my final year.

2025–2026

Story Machine AI

  • After Meta I started Story Machine AI, working on AI video.
  • Made a product that was great at putting videos together, and generated many videos with it, including a 45-minute movie about a year ago. (storymachine.ai)
  • User acquisition in the space was hard, so I tried a number of other ideas, including a game generation platform.
  • I couldn’t keep up with the coding agents as a small company, so I pivoted to making games for a bit and building a community.

2026

Making games again

  • That’s when I started Jamference, and it has been going much better.
  • The community is growing and some amazing games are coming out of it.
  • I make a game every round too: Kitty Kingdom was rated Most Fun, and Starry Meows won its round.
  • More of my games are on itch.io.

02

Jamference

A free AI game jam I founded and host. A new round starts every two weeks.

Over 100 games have been submitted to the Jamference game jams so far.

Each round pairs a theme with a mandatory design restriction, which pushes people away from the obvious use of AI and towards ideas that are new.
